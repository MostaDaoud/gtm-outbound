# Emails

Sequences, single sends, micro-lessons, and the offer mechanics that go in them. Every claim carries its lesson, timestamp, or source archive locator.

## 1. AIDA is the Email Default

Neville Medhora treats AIDA as the baseline working structure for direct email: "it's a very popular formula and it's super interesting, **especially for emails**" (*02-Copy Concepts > 02-The AIDA Model What It Is and How to Use It* @ 00:00).

**The method is mechanical:** Write the four words down the page, then fill them in: "I literally write out attention, interest, desire, action" (*01-Copywriting Course > 05-BONUS VIDEO* @ 33:47). This is a scaffold typed first, not an audit lens applied afterwards.

**The unit of planning is subject line + objective + body:** Medhora demonstrates an autoresponder sequence laid out explicitly this way: "subject line, objective, email copy; look: attention, interest, desire" (*01-Videos > 244-Follow this AIDA formula for everything you write* @ 05:44).

Naming a single **objective** per email forces each send to execute one job, preventing sequences from wandering into generic corporate newsletters.

## 2. The Micro-Lesson Architecture (Eddie Shleyner / VeryGoodCopy)

For recurring newsletters, nurture sequences, and relationship-driven broadcast emails, Eddie Shleyner's VeryGoodCopy architecture (2x Email Newsletter of the Year, 95,000+ subscribers) replaces standard promotional blasts with high-retention narrative micro-lessons.

### The 5-Beat Structure (See `vgc-principles.md` for full breakdown):
1. **Curiosity Subject Line:** Ends in a colon (`:`) or ellipsis, opening a high-intensity curiosity loop (e.g., `You are not alone:`, `The rules of "real" writing:`).
2. **In Media Res Hook:** Drops the reader straight into dialogue or action without greetings or throat-clearing pleasantries.
3. **Empathy Bridge:** Transitions from the narrative scene to a universal human conflict or marketing friction.
4. **Deconstructed Principle:** Outlines a specific mechanism using concrete before/after examples, numbered rules, or proof locators.
5. **Quiet CTA & Sign-off:** Soft, frictionless link to a service, masterclass, or consulting session, followed by an optional conversational P.S.

This format sustains 40%+ open rates over years because every issue functions as standalone entertainment and practical instruction.

## 3. Sequence Shapes

From John McIntyre's direct-response email architecture (*Drop Dead Copy, 03-John McIntyre AI Copy Secrets Vol 1* @ 49:07):

- **Welcome Sequence:** ~7 emails establishing origin story, core beliefs, expectations, and initial quick wins.
- **Promotional Launch Sequence:** ~5 emails building anticipation, introducing the offer, presenting proof, handling objections, and closing on deadline.
- **Customer Onboarding Sequence:** ~20 emails spaced over 60 days, driving feature adoption, celebrating milestones, and preventing churn.
- **Micro-Lesson Cadence (VGC Model):** 2 to 3 sends per week, each delivering a self-contained 300 to 700 word lesson.

Sequence design is bounded by clarity regarding each individual email's objective.

## 4. Deliverability and Formatting Basics

Medhora on typeface: use a universal font. "Arial is the font we should be sending our email in; generally that's accepted as a universal font that everyone has on their machines. If you use some fancy font, some people may not have it" (*01-Copywriting Course > 05-BONUS VIDEO* @ 33:47).

Email clients are not modern web browsers. Unrendered fonts fail silently and distort layout hierarchy. Keep plain-text formatting or clean, minimal HTML.

***

## 5. Preview Text is a Second Subject Line

Preview text is the secondary snippet displayed adjacent to the subject line in the inbox. Citing Litmus research: **up to 24% of subscribers evaluate the preview text before deciding to open** (*Product Marketing, 11-Competitive Intel & Market Research > 07-Competitive Newsletters for Product & the C-Suite* @ 11:07).

Never allow preview text to default to "View this email in your browser" or the opening greeting. Treat it as a supporting headline that answers or amplifies the curiosity loop established by the subject line.

## 6. Free Things Must Actually Be Free

Bart Schutz (*8. Pricing Psychology > 8.4 Special: Money, discounts & freebies* @ 14:13):

> "If you give away things it should be **totally free**. Even asking a telephone number and email address; that feels like I'm giving you something in return. You shouldn't do that, because then the reciprocity is gone... and the effect is gone."

Requiring an email address converts a gift into a transaction. Gated lead magnets capture addresses in exchange for perceived value, but do not generate psychological reciprocity. When genuine goodwill and brand affinity are the goal, provide ungated, zero-friction value.

***

## 7. Discount Timing: After, Not Before

Bart Schutz on reward timing: test "giving that free extra discount **after** people bought, as a delightful moment which is unexpected; boosting their experience, making them more loyal in the long run and **less price sensitive** because it was such a cool experience" (@ 14:13).

Pre-purchase discounting trains prospects to wait for markdowns. Post-purchase unexpected bonuses generate intense customer delight and loyalty.

***

## 8. Promotional Scarcity in Email

Scarcity in email sequences is scrutinized under repetition. Bart Schutz warns that repeated or artificial drip scarcity triggers customer resentment ("this is not fair... they start disliking you for it" *8.2* @ 32:47).

If an email sequence deploys scarcity or countdown deadlines, the restriction must be genuine, verifiable, and explained with a clear, logical reason (see `offers.md` §4).

***

## 9. Email Master Checklist

1. **Does every email have one named objective?** (§1)
2. **Are A / I / D / A or VGC Micro-Lesson beats followed cleanly?** (§1, §2)
3. **Does the subject line open a curiosity gap ending in a colon or punchy phrase?** (§2, `headlines.md`)
4. **Is preview text drafted as a dedicated secondary headline?** (§5)
5. **Are free resources truly free when reciprocity is the goal?** (§6)
6. **Is promotional scarcity authentic, logical, and truthful?** (§8)
7. **Is the font universal (e.g., Arial, Georgia) and mobile-responsive?** (§4)
8. **Are all em-dashes removed in favor of colons, parentheses, or periods?**
