## Headlines

**Curiosity beating authority.** Brian Massey's AdWords test, cited by Schutz.
The conventional version claimed roughly *we hold all the secrets to internet
marketing*. The winner:

> **"lousy marketing and dated IDs — don't come to our site"**
> (Schutz, *3. Attention & Perception > 3.3 Attention* @ 11:10)

*Why:* disqualifies the reader, inverts the expected sales posture, opens a gap
instead of closing one.

**The story in one line.**

> **"They laughed when I sat down to play"**
> (*Digital Psychology & Persuasion, 18-Cognitive Biases > 11-Narrative Fallacy*
> @ 00:38, paraphrased on the recording)

*Why:* character, conflict, implied reversal, unresolved ending — seven words.

**Voice as the differentiator.**

> **"It's like milk but made for humans."**
> Body: *"so what is this oat drink anyway? Milk? No, it's not milk."*
> (Oatly, cited in *Branding & Brand Strategy, 14-Branding > 05-Build a brand
> voice* @ 01:28)

*Why:* the lesson's point is what it refused — "definitely not a safe choice for
the target audience." Legible as that brand before you see the logo.

**A researched value proposition that refuses cleverness.**

> **"Shop the world's largest selection of built-to-last, weathertight pet
> doors."**
> (*Product Marketing, 13-Radical Differentiation >
> 11-everyone-hates-marketers__value-proposition-what-is* @ 44:37)

*Why:* "pet doors" names the product, "largest selection" is the surveyed
choice reason, and "built-to-last, weathertight" carries the outcomes prospects
wanted and customers reported receiving (@ 45:24). Nothing decorative is left.

## Positioning lines

**Proof plus stakes, without naming the competitor.** A Drift page:

> *"50,000 other businesses are growing faster as a result of using Drift. How
> much money are you leaving on the table?"*
> (*Product Marketing, 12-Positioning > 08-How to Create a Memorable Simple
> Promise* @ 10:18)

*Why:* social proof, then a change in stakes, then the CTA — and "they're not
talking about Intercom here." Positioning against the status quo, not the rival.

**A definition you can hand a client who wants "differentiation".**

> *"A business strategy disguised as a positioning method to make your brand the
> only choice in a specific category for a specific group of people."*
> (*Product Marketing, 13-Radical Differentiation >
> 02-Intro to Radical Differentiation* @ 02:23)

*Why:* it sets the price of the thing they are asking for in the same breath as
defining it — the same lesson adds "it requires a lot of sacrifice… a long-term
commitment across the board, across the entire experience."

**Oversized ambition on an unglamorous category.**

> Sweetgreen: *"to inspire healthier communities by connecting people to real
> food"* — founded by students with "a vision to change the world for salad. You
> never think that you could change the world for the salad, but they did it."
> (*14-Branding > 03-Brand soul* @ 05:27)

## Cold email

**What not to send.** Medhora's example of the awkward cold email:

> ✗ *"Hey, do you want to make tons of money? If you don't act right now, you'll
> never get my special secrets."*
> (*03-Copywriting Guides > 13-Common fixes to your copy mistakes we see everyday*
> @ 07:36)

**The instruction that replaces it:**

> *"Aim to offer a simple, obvious value up front and not engage in a wild sales
> pitch. The result will be better relationships and better conversations."*
> (@ 07:36)

**A four-email outreach set**, from research (Rohan, *Module 1 > Video_3-How To Do
Research* @ 09:24):

1. **Value email** based on a specific piece of their content, with a PS about
   your offer
2. **Testimonial / case study email** — take a testimonial from their sales page
   and say "if you want similar results then join my program"
3. **General angle** — the *crossroads*: "option A, try to figure out everything
   on your own, spend months on trial and error; option B, join my program"

*Why the crossroads works:* it makes the status quo an explicit choice with a
stated cost. See `positioning.md` §1.

## Pricing and offers

**Three-tier rate ladder.** Medhora's rule of thumb:

> *"You triple it and you triple it again. So a $500 project could potentially be
> a $1,500 project pretty easily. And the $500 price doesn't scare away the people
> on the low end. But then if someone wants a much more premium product, they're
> also likely to get this... this price should be at least 10 times more than this
> price."*
> (*01-Videos > 66-Office Hours September 18 2025* @ 10:44)

*Why:* the low anchor preserves accessibility; the top tier captures buyers who
would otherwise have been capped at your only price — "if you just charge $500,
people go, okay, that's the price. That's all you'll ever make."

**The ugly brother, applied to an existing tier.** Usabilla raised the price of
its *smallest* plan from 50 to 80 against a 90 middle tier. Conversion flat,
average order value up (*Schutz, 1.4 System 2 Persuasion Techniques* @ 23:10).

**Scarcity, full recipe.** Limited availability + reasoned scarcity + active
competition + a discount + loss framing — "emphasize the loss of it, because that
is more emotional than the gain of acting now" (*7.2 Techniques for Your Offer*
@ 33:57).

**Free must be free.** *"if you give away things it should be totally free. Even
asking a telephone number and email address — that feels like I'm giving you
something in return... then the reciprocity is gone"* (*8.4* @ 14:13).

## Conversion results worth citing

| Change | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Forced-choice lightbox vs header/footer for cookie consent | opt-in ~20% → **75%** | Schutz *1.4* @ 04:47 |
| Emphasising one interest rate instead of two | beat full redesigns from two rival agencies | *1.4* @ 13:31 |
| Same 8 USPs, chunked into 3 layered groups | won | *1.4* @ 15:13 |
| **Reducing** the number of filters (Conrad) | won | *1.4* @ 26:09 |
| Removing "loan" / "credit", emphasising new kitchen or car | higher conversion across all tests | *4.5* @ 37:57 |
| Opt-out vs opt-in organ donation | 85–99.97% vs far lower | *1.4* @ 01:11 |
| Adding "your contribution is needed" style social norm to UK tax letters | **$9bn** additional | *21-Nonconscious Motivation > 04-Social Proof* @ 01:18 |
| Short guarantee ("you may cancel at any time") vs a longer, warmer one | short won by **14%** | *18-Cognitive Biases > 09-Fluency Bias* @ 01:48 |
| Removing a section from a long-form page | sales **+20%** | *03-Digital Psychology  Persuasion Minidegree > 02-Principles of Persuasive Design* @ 25:53 |
| Adding story to a sales deck | completion **20% → 60%** | *12-Positioning > 02-The 8 Elements of Your Positioning Story* @ 08:35 |

**The pattern:** six of these ten wins were **subtractive or re-emphasising** —
cutting a section, dropping a word, shortening a guarantee, reducing filters,
chunking what was already there. Only the tax letter, the sales deck and the
forced-choice lightbox added anything. Worth having ready when a client asks for
more.

**The counter-pattern worth naming too:** the three additive wins all added
*narrative or social context*, not features or claims. Nothing on this list was
won by describing the product harder.

## Timing and process

**Pitch the upsell after a win.**

> *"usually you want to pitch the upsell after a win — after you have wrote them
> an email sequence that made them money or after you wrote them a youtube script
> that got them a ton of views... that's when their dopamine is at the high,
> that's when they're most in the happiest state to give your upsell a chance."*
> (Rohan, *Module 3 Client Multiplier > Video_5* @ 18:33)

**Discount after purchase, not before.** *"giving that free extra discount after
people bought, as a delightful moment which is unexpected — boosting their
experience, making them more loyal in the long run and less price sensitive"*
(*Schutz, 8.4* @ 14:13).

**On niching.**

> *"don't niche down, the universe will niche down for you... people will tell you
> 'I'm going to pay you a lot of money for the skill that you have.'"*
> (Medhora, *01-Videos > 629-April 20th 2023* @ 57:17)

*Note the disagreement worth preserving:* this sits against the Product Marketing
material on choosing a **minimum viable market** deliberately
(`positioning.md` §4). Medhora is talking about a freelancer discovering a niche
from market feedback; the positioning courses are talking about a company
choosing one. Both are defensible in their own context — do not merge them.

## Practice prompt

Shiv Shetti's self-training prompt, usable verbatim (*08-Shiv Shetti AI Copy
Secrets Vol 1* @ 09:41) — reproduced in full in `ai-workflow.md` §4. Structure:
ask for a **challenging client brief**, write against it, request **graded
per-section feedback**, and specify the difficulty axis (market saturation,
levels of awareness, positioning).
