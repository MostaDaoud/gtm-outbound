# Critique Rubric

The 100-point scoring system for assessing copy. Synthesised from the other
references; every criterion traces to a sourced principle, cited so a score can
be defended rather than asserted.

**How to use it.** Score each category, then report findings by severity, not by
category order. A single Critical finding matters more than a spread of Lows.
Always name the principle behind a deduction — "this is vague" is an opinion,
"this states a benefit without the evidence the reader needs to believe it" is a
finding.

---

## Category 1 — Audience & Psychology (25 points)

**1.1 Correct system targeted (8)**
Does the copy match how this decision is actually made? Check the three
conditions that summon System 2 — importance, justification, knowledge
(`psychology.md` §1; Schutz *1.2* @ 04:09). An essay in front of an impulse
purchase, or a slogan in front of a considered one, is a mismatch.

**1.2 Written for a situation, not a personality (6)**
"We highly overemphasize personalities and underemphasize the situations"
(Schutz *6.1* @ 18:30). Deduct for personas built from self-reported traits with
no situational grounding (`voice.md` §7).

**1.3 Status quo addressed (6)**
Is the real competitor — doing nothing — named and dislodged? "It's the status
quo, not your competitors, that's the biggest impediment to change"
(*12-Positioning > 02* @ 02:58; `positioning.md` §1).

Deduct here too for a **demand-type mismatch**: a new concept sold as though the
category were established. If nobody has budgeted for the thing, comparison and
differentiation land on nothing and the copy has to teach first
(*12-Positioning > 05-How to Identify Your Demand Type* @ 00:00;
`positioning.md` §4).

**1.4 Objection placement (5)**
Reassurance appears where the objection is already live, not on cold entry.
Raising a fear plants it: the telco page cut conversion for prospects while
lifting renewal for existing customers (Schutz *2.1* @ 42:11;
`sales-pages.md` §4).

## Category 2 — Structure & Clarity (20 points)

**2.1 Identifiable framework (6)**
AIDA, story spine, or a deliberate alternative — present and in order
(`frameworks.md` §1–2).

**2.2 Belief chain complete (6)**
Morrison's five beliefs, each established somewhere: they get me; they can help;
I understand the path; I want it; I believe I'll get the result
(*Words Are Wands, 06-Bonus > 02* @ 02:38; `sales-pages.md` §1). Deduct per
missing belief.

**2.3 Each screen stands alone, and the scan layer carries it (4)**
System 1 reasons only from what is visible — "what you see is all there is"
(Schutz *2.1* @ 45:21). Deduct where meaning depends on a paragraph below the
fold.

Two concrete triggers, from the persuasive-design rule that scanning is "the most
dominant behavior online" (*03-Digital Psychology  Persuasion Minidegree > 02* @
16:03; `headlines.md` §7):

- More than **two paragraphs** without a sub-headline.
- Sub-headings that only label sections ("Features", "How it works") rather than
  advancing the argument. Read the headline and sub-headings alone: if the case
  does not survive, the layer most people actually read is empty.

**2.4 Focal claims limited (4)**
One to three USPs, five maximum; chunk beyond that (Schutz *1.4* @ 11:36).
Deduct for undifferentiated feature lists.

---

## Category 3 — Evidence & Specificity (20 points)

**3.1 Claims carry evidence (8)**
Every load-bearing claim has a number, a named source, or a concrete example.
Deduct hardest for unsupported superlatives.

**3.2 End state, not instrument (6)**
Does the copy name what the reader gets, or what you deliver? Removing "loan"
and "credit" and naming the new kitchen raised conversion in every test
(Schutz *4.5* @ 37:57; `offers.md` §6).

**3.3 Information gain (6)**
Is there something here that could not have been generated without this
writer — original data, first-hand experience, a genuinely new angle?
"Everyone has ChatGPT now. What's something new you're bringing to the table?"
(Meng *02 > 07* @ 01:14; `ai-workflow.md` §1). This is the hardest criterion to
pass and the most valuable.

---

## Category 4 — Voice & Distinctiveness (15 points)

**4.1 Would not work on a competitor's page (6)**
The swap test. If the logo could change and the copy still fit, it is doing work
for the category (`voice.md` §2; `headlines.md` §4).

**4.2 Register matches positioning (5)**
"If your brand is luxury, you need to choose a luxury identity"
(*14-Branding > 06* @ 01:20). Deduct for direct-response cadence on premium
positioning and vice versa.

Separate voice failures from tone failures before deducting: personality is
stable, tone is "the mood your brand is in at the moment of writing," and it is
*supposed* to vary by topic, audience and channel (*14-Branding > 05* @ 07:24;
`voice.md` §1). A sombre incident notice from a playful brand is correct, not a
defect. Two specific triggers that are real: **rhythm** that does not match the
brand's established pace, and **hardline selling aimed at an audience that
rejects it** — the lesson names European audiences explicitly.

**4.3 Clichés the audience hates are absent (4)**
From the differentiation exercise: "what are the clichés that they hate"
(*13-Radical Differentiation > 08* @ 03:52; `positioning.md` §4).

## Category 5 — Offer & Action (20 points)

**5.1 Opening opens a gap (5)**
Curiosity over confidence — "lousy marketing and dated IDs, don't come to our
site" beat the authority claim (Schutz *3.3* @ 11:10; `headlines.md` §1).
Deduct for headlines that summarise.

**5.2 CTA sits with the emotional peak (5)**
"Display your USPs and your call to actions close to those emotionally powerful
spots on your page" — and use visuals, which carry emotion better than text
(Schutz *3.4* @ 23:42; `sales-pages.md` §2).

**5.3 Offer structure deliberate (5)**
Price structure chosen before the number; tiers with an intentional relative
value story; drip pricing consolidated into one surcharge with the total always
visible (`offers.md` §1–2, §7).

**5.4 Scarcity honest and complete (5)**
If used: limited availability, **reasoned**, active competition, an extrinsic
motivator, loss framing (*7.2* @ 33:57). **Deduct the full 5 for invented
scarcity** — the corpus's own instructors decline to manufacture the underlying
facts (*1.4* @ 18:07; `client-work.md` §2).

## Severity bands

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | The page cannot convert as written — no offer, no evidence, structural failure, or a dishonest claim |
| **High** | A named principle is violated in a way that measurably costs conversion |
| **Medium** | A real improvement with a clear fix |
| **Low** | Polish |

**Fabricated evidence, invented scarcity, or manufactured social proof are always
Critical**, regardless of score elsewhere. That is the one place this rubric does
not trade off.

---

## Score interpretation

| Score | Reading |
|---|---|
| 90–100 | Ship |
| 75–89 | Ship after High findings are fixed |
| 60–74 | Structurally sound, needs an evidence and specificity pass |
| 40–59 | Rewrite against a framework |
| < 40 | Positioning problem wearing a copy costume — go back to `positioning.md` |

That last band is the most common misdiagnosis. If the copy cannot be fixed by
rewriting sentences, the fault is upstream.

## Delivering a critique

1. **Score, then rank findings by severity** — not by category.
2. **Quote the line** you are marking. No finding without a quotation.
3. **Name the principle and its source**, so the writer can check you.
4. **Give the rewritten line**, not just the objection.
5. **Preserve disagreement.** Where instructors conflict — Medhora's "don't niche
   down" against the minimum-viable-market discipline (`swipe-file.md`) — say so
   rather than picking a side silently.
6. **Calibrate score confidence to the brief.** With the audience, offer,
   objective, channel, and evidence available, report the score as high
   confidence (approximately ±3). If one or two are missing, mark it provisional
   (approximately ±7). If most are missing, state assumptions and use a broad
   range (approximately ±12) instead of false precision.

### Fast repair pass: the seven sweeps

After scoring, repair in this order: **clarity; voice and tone; so what; prove
it; specificity; heightened emotion; zero risk**. This comes from Joanna
Wiebe's "seven sweeps," where each pass asks one question instead of attempting
a full rewrite (*Product Marketing, 13-Radical Differentiation >
11-everyone-hates-marketers__3-steps-to-write-copy-tha* @ 48:07–50:12).

The sweeps map onto this rubric without changing its weights: clarity → Category
2; voice and tone → Category 4; so what, proof, and specificity → Category 3;
heightened emotion → Categories 1 and 5; zero risk → Category 5. Rerun clarity
last, because every repair can make the copy harder to understand.
