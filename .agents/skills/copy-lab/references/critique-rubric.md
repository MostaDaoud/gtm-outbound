# Critique Rubric

The 100-point scoring system for assessing copy. Synthesized from the core
reference library, classic direct-response canon, Eddie Shleyner's VeryGoodCopy
and Transformational Landing Pages systems, and local sources. Every criterion
traces to a sourced principle, cited so a score can be defended rather than asserted.

**How to use it.** Score each category, then report findings by severity, not by
category order. A single Critical finding matters more than a spread of Lows.
Always name the principle behind a deduction: "this is vague" is an opinion,
"this states a benefit without the evidence the reader needs to believe it" is a
finding.

---

## Category 1: Audience & Psychology (25 points)

**1.1 Correct system targeted (7)**
Does the copy match how this decision is actually made? Check the three
conditions that summon System 2: importance, justification, knowledge
(`psychology.md` §1; Schutz *1.2* @ 04:09). An essay in front of an impulse
purchase, or a slogan in front of a considered one, is a mismatch.

**1.2 Written for a situation, not a personality & Person (sans "a") (6)**
"We highly overemphasize personalities and underemphasize the situations"
(Schutz *6.1* @ 18:30). Deduct for personas built from self-reported traits with
no situational grounding (`voice.md` §7).
Apply Eddie Shleyner's Person (sans "a") principle (`landing-pages.md` §1;
`vgc-principles.md` §7): copy must be grounded in real customer interview data
using Chris Voss mirroring techniques, not hypothetical demographic fluff.

**1.3 Status quo addressed & Ed Mayer's 40-40-20 Rule (6)**
Is the real competitor: doing nothing: named and dislodged? "It's the status
quo, not your competitors, that's the biggest impediment to change"
(*12-Positioning > 02* @ 02:58; `positioning.md` §1).
Check Ed Mayer's 40-40-20 Rule (`vgc-principles.md` §1): 40% Audience/Market,
40% Offer, 20% Creative. If the copy targets the wrong market or sells to cold
traffic with warm assumptions, the creative cannot compensate.
Deduct here too for a **demand-type mismatch**: a new concept sold as though the
category were established (*12-Positioning > 05* @ 00:00; `positioning.md` §4).

**1.4 Objection placement, Transitional Congruence & LISH Heuristic (6)**
Reassurance appears where the objection is already live, not on cold entry.
Raising a fear plants it: the telco page cut conversion for prospects while
lifting renewal for existing customers (Schutz *2.1* @ 42:11; `sales-pages.md` §4).
Check **Transitional Congruence** (`landing-pages.md` §1): the traffic source
(ad, cold email, social post) must mirror the destination Hero divider in copy,
tone, and visual promise with 1:1 fidelity.
Apply the **LISH Heuristic (Length Implies Strength Heuristic)** (`landing-pages.md` §6;
`psychology.md` §2): where prospect skepticism is high, check whether social
proof density is sufficient to trigger System 1's automatic conclusion:
"So many people cannot possibly be wrong."

## Category 2: Structure & Clarity (20 points)

**2.1 Identifiable framework & Architecture (6)**
The structure must be present, deliberate, and in order:
- For long-form sales pages: AIDA, PAS, or story spine (`frameworks.md` §1-2).
- For landing pages: Eddie Shleyner's 7 Dividers and CDCA Engine (`landing-pages.md` §2-3):
  Divider 1: Hero [Clarity + Action]
  Divider 2: Proof & Risk Reversal [Credibility + Action]
  Divider 3: Fast-Action Conversion [Action + Credibility]
  Divider 4: The Fascination Wall [Desire + Action]
  Divider 5: Mid-Funnel Proof [Credibility + Action]
  Divider 6: Massive Social Proof Wall [Credibility + Action]
  Divider 7: Final Conversion & Reassurance [Action + Credibility]
- For editorial newsletters: Eddie Shleyner's 5-Beat Micro-Lesson Architecture
  (`vgc-principles.md` §5; `emails.md` §2): in media res opening, drama/conflict,
  cognitive breakthrough, practical application, and punchy callout.
- For sales letters: Collier's six-part letter or Schwab's five-part advertisement
  (`classic-direct-response.md`).
- For social posts (LinkedIn, X, Threads): The 4-Format Hook Architecture (`social-hooks.md` §4):
  Format 1: Dense (140-160 chars, continuous prose, payload before char 140).
  Format 2: Punchy + Context (Line 1 <= 50 chars, blank line, Line 2 <= 45 chars in landing mode, or trailing mode passing the Cut-Zone Test).
  Format 3: Single-Line Bomb (<= 45 chars, 0 breaks).
  Format 4: Stacked (tight vs spaced, matched line widths).

**2.2 Belief chain complete & The 6-Element Hero Protocol (6)**
Morrison's five beliefs, each established somewhere: they get me; they can help;
I understand the path; I want it; I believe I'll get the result
(*Words Are Wands, 06-Bonus > 02* @ 02:38; `sales-pages.md` §1).
For landing page hero sections, audit against the **6-Element Hero Protocol**
(`landing-pages.md` §4):
1. Primer copy ending in a colon (`:`) to initiate Joe Sugarman's Slippery Slide.
2. Hero headline enforcing Eugene Schwartz's Clean Window Principle: 3 to 7 words,
   zero cleverness, looking straight through the words at the product.
3. Future pacing subhead using active trigger verbs (*Stop feeling [pain]... Start [desire]...*).
4. Hero body copy busting the primary friction point in 2 to 4 sentences.
5. Urgent CTA button pairing economic incentive with a parenthetical deadline.
6. Concrete visual deliverable artifact with a contrast discount or guarantee badge.
Deduct 1 to 2 points per missing element in a landing page hero.

**2.3 Each screen stands alone, scan layer, and Fascination Wall (4)**
System 1 reasons only from what is visible: "what you see is all there is"
(Schutz *2.1* @ 45:21). Deduct where meaning depends on a paragraph below the fold.
Check the two concrete scanning triggers (*03-Digital Psychology & Persuasion Minidegree > 02*
@ 16:03; `headlines.md` §7):
- More than two paragraphs without a sub-headline.
- Sub-headings that only label sections rather than advancing the case.
For bullet and teaser sections, enforce the **Fascination Wall checkerboard grid**
(`landing-pages.md` §5; `fascination-bullets.md`): fascinations must alternate syntax
across the 18 filters in a two-column grid to defeat reader fatigue.
For social posts and feed copy, audit against the **Mobile Truncation Budget & Cut-Zone Test**
(`social-hooks.md` §2-3): full visible line <= 50 characters (~90 units); last visible line <= 45
characters (~78 units, reserving space for `...more`); trailing hooks must pass the Cut-Zone
Test at characters 42 and 66 with both clear value context and an unresolved structural element.

**2.4 Focal claims limited & Anti-Description (4)**
One to three USPs, five maximum; chunk beyond that (Schutz *1.4* @ 11:36).
Apply Eugene Schwartz's Mind Movie Method and Eddie Shleyner's Anti-Description
(`vgc-principles.md` §2): prose must remain intentionally sparse, creating cognitive
"holes" for the reader's imagination to project themselves into the transformation,
rather than clogging the mental screen with dense, decorative adjective soup.

## Category 3: Evidence & Specificity (20 points)

**3.1 Claims carry evidence & Gary Bencivenga Proof First (8)**
Every load-bearing claim has a number, a named source, or a concrete example.
Deduct hardest for unsupported superlatives.
Enforce **Gary Bencivenga's Proof First Doctrine** (`landing-pages.md` §6):
putting hard evidence (statistics, media logos, client badges, guarantee seals)
before the pitch (Divider 2) disarms skepticism early. Deduct when proof is
delayed until the bottom of the page while unproven assertions multiply.

**3.2 End state, not instrument & Whale vs. School Testimonials (6)**
Does the copy name what the reader gets, or what you deliver? Removing "loan"
and "credit" and naming the new kitchen raised conversion in every test
(Schutz *4.5* @ 37:57; `offers.md` §6).
Audit social proof against the **Whale vs. School Testimonial Architecture**
(`landing-pages.md` §6):
- **Whale Testimonials**: High-authority case studies from recognized industry
  leaders must feature full name, title, headshot, and specific metrics achieved.
  Deduct for generic praise lacking measurable outcomes.
- **School Testimonials**: Short, punchy 1 to 2 sentence snippets from everyday
  users arranged in rows or dense strips to build aggregate social momentum.

**3.3 Information gain & The 9 Guardrails for Fascinations (6)**
Is there something here that could not have been generated without this
writer: original data, first-hand experience, a genuinely new angle?
"Everyone has ChatGPT now. What's something new you're bringing to the table?"
(Meng *02 > 07* @ 01:14; `ai-workflow.md` §1).
For curiosity bullets and fascinations, audit against the **9 Guardrails and the
Parris Lampropoulos 7x rule** (`vgc-principles.md` §4; `landing-pages.md` §5):
draft 7x what you keep; enforce benefit specificity; prove every claim; ensure
clarity over concision; and ground every tease in verifiable page or module locators.

## Category 4: Voice & Distinctiveness (15 points)

**4.1 Would not work on a competitor's page & The 3 Rules of Real Writing (6)**
The swap test: if the logo could change and the copy still fit, it is doing work
for the category (`voice.md` §2; `headlines.md` §4).
Enforce Eddie Shleyner's **3 Rules of "Real" Writing** (`vgc-principles.md` §3):
1. Never let the reader inside the mind of a character (actions and dialogue only;
   show concrete behavior rather than reporting inner thoughts).
2. Avoid over-describing setting or characters.
3. Write in the present tense to simulate live drama.
Deduct for narrative copy that bogs down in internal monologue or decorative scene-painting.

**4.2 Register matches positioning & Language Economy (5)**
"If your brand is luxury, you need to choose a luxury identity"
(*14-Branding > 06* @ 01:20). Deduct for direct-response cadence on premium
positioning and vice versa.
Audit for **The Fast Feeling & Language Economy** (`vgc-principles.md` §6):
active verbs driving sentences, decorative Latinate adjectives pruned, and the
**Rule of 26** (sentence burstiness: alternating punchy 3-word sentences with
rhythmic 15-word explanatory clauses). Deduct for sluggish, adjective-laden phrasing.

**4.3 Clichés the audience hates & The 9 Compelling Sentence Rules (4)**
From the differentiation exercise: "what are the clichés that they hate"
(*13-Radical Differentiation > 08* @ 03:52; `positioning.md` §4).
Audit against Eddie Shleyner's **9 Rules of a Compelling Sentence** (`vgc-principles.md` §4):
- **Strict Ban on Work Words**: Deduct for words that signal mental labor (*learn,
  study, memorize, master*). Replace with payoff verbs (*discover, uncover, unlock*).
- **Strict Ban on Transaction Words**: Deduct for friction words (*buy, purchase,
  pay, order*). Replace with value verbs (*get, claim, access, join*).
- **Anglo-Saxon Simplicity**: Replace multi-syllabic Latinate abstractions with
  simple, visceral Anglo-Saxon phrasing.
- **Strict Ban on Em-Dashes**: Strictly flag em-dashes as an immediate mechanical tell.
  Replace with colons, parentheses, commas, or separate sentences.
- **Strict Ban on Setup Cliches**: In social hooks and feed openers (`social-hooks.md` §6),
  deduct for throat-clearing setup language (*In this post..., I want to share..., Here is what
  I learned...*) or generic buzzwords (*game-changer, let that sink in, read that again, I am excited to share*).

## Category 5: Offer & Action (20 points)

**5.1 Opening opens a gap & Primer ending in colon (5)**
Curiosity over confidence: "lousy marketing and dated IDs, don't come to our
site" beat the authority claim (Schutz *3.3* @ 11:10; `headlines.md` §1).
Deduct for headlines that summarize instead of pulling the reader forward.
Audit for the **Slippery Slide Primer Rule** (`landing-pages.md` §4; `vgc-principles.md` §5):
the opening primer copy must end in a colon (`:`) to pull the reader down
Joe Sugarman's slide into the headline and body copy.

**5.2 CTA sits with the emotional peak & 1 CTA Per Divider (5)**
"Display your USPs and your call to actions close to those emotionally powerful
spots on your page" (Schutz *3.4* @ 23:42; `sales-pages.md` §2).
Enforce the **1 CTA Per Divider Rule** (`landing-pages.md` §7): every major section
or visual divider must offer an anchor back to conversion.
Audit for **Parenthetical Urgency & Economic Incentive** (`landing-pages.md` §7):
conversion buttons must pair the core action verb with a parenthetical economic
benefit or deadline (e.g., `Get Instant Access (Save 50% - Offer ends Friday)`).
Deduct for bare, transactional button copy (*"Submit"*, *"Click Here"*).

**5.3 Offer structure deliberate & The Frictionless 3-Field Rule (5)**
Price structure chosen before the number; tiers with an intentional relative
value story; drip pricing consolidated into one surcharge with the total always
visible (`offers.md` §1-2, §7).
Enforce the **Frictionless 3-Field Rule** (`landing-pages.md` §7): never ask for
more than 3 fields on opt-in or lead capture forms (Name, Work Email, Company).
Deduct 2 points for every unnecessary field that introduces checkout or lead friction.

**5.4 Scarcity honest, complete & Risk-Reversal Guarantee Seal (5)**
If used: limited availability, **reasoned**, active competition, an extrinsic
motivator, loss framing (*7.2* @ 33:57). **Deduct the full 5 for invented
scarcity** - the corpus's own instructors decline to manufacture the underlying
facts (*1.4* @ 18:07; `client-work.md` §2).
Enforce the **Risk-Reversal Guarantee Seal** (`landing-pages.md` §7): a clear,
prominent 30-day or 60-day guarantee seal must sit directly adjacent to the
conversion form and CTA button to eliminate checkout anxiety.

## Severity bands

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Critical** | The page cannot convert as written: no offer, no evidence, structural failure, or a dishonest claim |
| **High** | A named principle is violated in a way that measurably costs conversion |
| **Medium** | A real improvement with a clear fix |
| **Low** | Polish |

**Fabricated evidence, invented scarcity, or manufactured social proof are always
Critical**, regardless of score elsewhere. That is the one place this rubric does
not trade off.

---

## Score interpretation

| Score | Reading |
|---|---|
| 90-100 | Ship |
| 75-89 | Ship after High findings are fixed |
| 60-74 | Structurally sound, needs an evidence and specificity pass |
| 40-59 | Rewrite against a framework |
| < 40 | Positioning problem wearing a copy costume: go back to `positioning.md` |

That last band is the most common misdiagnosis. If the copy cannot be fixed by
rewriting sentences, the fault is upstream.

## Delivering a critique

1. **Score, then rank findings by severity**: not by category.
2. **Quote the line** you are marking. No finding without a quotation.
3. **Name the principle and its source**, so the writer can check you.
4. **Give the rewritten line**, not just the objection.
5. **Preserve disagreement.** Where instructors conflict: Medhora's "don't niche
   down" against the minimum-viable-market discipline (`swipe-file.md`): say so
   rather than picking a side silently.
6. **Calibrate score confidence to the brief.** With the audience, offer,
   objective, channel, and evidence available, report the score as high
   confidence (approximately ±3). If one or two are missing, mark it provisional
   (approximately ±7). If most are missing, state assumptions and use a broad
   range (approximately ±12) instead of false precision.

### Fast repair pass: the seven sweeps and three editing passes

After scoring, repair in this order: **clarity; voice and tone; so what; prove
it; specificity; heightened emotion; zero risk**. This comes from Joanna
Wiebe's "seven sweeps," where each pass asks one question instead of attempting
a full rewrite (*Product Marketing, 13-Radical Differentiation >
11-everyone-hates-marketers__3-steps-to-write-copy-tha* @ 48:07-50:12).

Combine with Eddie Shleyner's **Three Editing Passes** (`vgc-principles.md` §6):
1. **The "Fast Feeling" Pass**: delete unnecessary words, shorten paragraphs,
   and inject Anglo-Saxon active verbs.
2. **The Anti-Description Pass**: convert abstract adjectives into observable
   actions and sensory moments.
3. **The 9-Rule Sentence Pass**: purge all work words (*learn, study*), transaction
   words (*buy, pay*), and mechanical tells (em-dashes).
