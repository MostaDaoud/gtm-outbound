# VeryGoodCopy Principles: The Eddie Shleyner Canon

A systematic direct-response methodology distilled from 484 email micro-lessons, 324,000 words, and 12 years of publishing VeryGoodCopy (2x Email Newsletter of the Year, 95,000+ subscribers). Grounded in classical direct response (Eugene Schwartz, Claude Hopkins, Joe Sugarman, Gary Bencivenga, and Ed Mayer) and honed for contemporary digital channels.

***

## 1. The Foundation: Ed Mayer's 40-40-20 Rule

Direct-marketing success is determined by three variables:

- **40% Market:** Reaching the right audience with acute desire or pain.
- **40% Offer:** Presenting the right product, price, terms, and risk reversal.
- **20% Creative:** Copywriting, visual layout, and formatting.

As Richard Sears (founder of Sears, Roebuck & Co.) stated: *"I can write the ad on a paper bag in crayon, but if it is the right offer for the right person at the right time, it will sell."*

### The Brian Kurtz Corollory
Publisher Brian Kurtz (Boardroom Inc., who mailed hundreds of millions of direct-mail letters) clarified the creative role: *"The copy is the least important... until it is not. Once you have your audience and your offer dialed in, the messaging, the copy, becomes the most important."*

When competing in mature markets with similar offers, copy is the deciding variable between failure and category dominance.

## 2. The "Mind Movie" Method & Anti-Description

Anti-description is an intentional literary and conversion technique. By deliberately stripping away decorative prose and excessive detail, the copywriter leaves cognitive "holes" that the reader instinctively fills using their own memory and imagination.

### The Cognitive Mechanism (Eugene Schwartz)
In *The Brilliance Breakthrough*, Eugene Schwartz observed that *"it is easier to decode a picture into words than it is to encode words into a picture."*

The human brain processes imagery, not raw phonetics. Over-describing a scene forces the reader to decode adjectives and analyze grammar, pulling them into analytical System 2 thinking. Anti-description presents concrete actions and dialogue, allowing System 1 to project a clear mental movie immediately.

### The 3 Rules of "Real" Writing
To build an authentic mental movie, follow three strict rules:

1. **Never let the reader inside the mind of a character:**  
   Do not explain internal thoughts or emotions (e.g., avoid *"He felt terrified and uncertain"*). Show only verifiable actions and spoken words (e.g., *"His heel hit the wall. He picked at his cuticle until it bled."*). Let the reader infer the emotion.
2. **Avoid giving too much information:**  
   Do not describe background decor, eye colors, or weather unless it advances the narrative tension. Excess detail clogs the reader's imagination.
3. **Use the present tense:**  
   Present-tense prose (*"I say"*, *"She turns"*, *"The monitor beeps"*) creates live, unfolding drama. The reader experiences the events in real time.

## 3. The 9 Rules of a Compelling Sentence (Fascinations)

Internet marketing pioneer Ken McCarthy called writing fascinations *"the single-most important copywriting skill."* If you can write a compelling fascination, you can write headlines, email subject lines, subheads, and video hooks.

Eddie Shleyner identifies 9 mandatory rules for drafting compelling sentences:

- **Rule 1: Write more than you need.**  
  Quality is the product of volume. Follow Parris Lampropoulos's direct-mail practice: write 7 times more fascinations than your asset requires (e.g., draft 70 bullets to select the best 10).
- **Rule 2: Prove it with specificity.**  
  Every fascination must answer the prospect's silent question: *"What is in it for me?"* Replace vague claims with precise, tangible outcomes.
- **Rule 3: Prove it with a source.**  
  Anchor bold claims to named practitioners, documented tests, or module locators.
- **Rule 4: Clarity beats concision.**  
  Do not sacrifice understanding for the sake of brevity. A long, two-sentence fascination that explains a mechanism converts better than a short, vague one-liner.
- **Rule 5: Use simple words.**  
  Never force the reader to pause to decode vocabulary. Use plain Anglo-Saxon words. Complex words trigger analysis; simple words evoke emotion.
- **Rule 6: Use the prospect's language.**  
  Reflect the exact phrases, complaints, and slang spoken by target buyers during customer interviews.
- **Rule 7: Avoid transactional words.**  
  Never use *buy*, *purchase*, or *order* inside bullets. Keep the prospect focused entirely on the transformation, not the extraction of cash.
- **Rule 8: Avoid work words.**  
  Never use *learn*, *study*, or *master*. Prospects resist cognitive effort. Use discovery verbs: *find out*, *uncover*, *discover*, *see how*.
- **Rule 9: Create variance.**  
  Avoid rhythmic monotony. Alternate sentence lengths and switch across the 18 fascination filters (Mechanisms, What Never, Right/Wrong, Why, How, If/Then).

## 4. The Micro-Lesson Email Architecture

VeryGoodCopy pioneered the high-retention "Micro-Lesson" newsletter format. It repudiates self-indulgent personal updates and corporate throat-clearing, delivering immediate value in 300 to 700 words.

### The 5-Beat Structural Blueprint

```text
[ BEAT 1: Subject Line + Colon ]
Intriguing, curiosity-driven statement ending in a colon (:) or ellipsis.

[ BEAT 2: The In Media Res Drop ]
Open directly inside an active scene, confrontation, or dialogue exchange.
Zero pleasantries ("Hope your week is going well"). Zero setup.

[ BEAT 3: The Empathy Bridge ]
Pivot smoothly from the personal narrative to a universal human or copywriting conflict.
Introduce the core psychological friction.

[ BEAT 4: The Deconstructed Mechanism ]
Break down the technique with concrete rules, before/after examples, or numbered steps.
Present the principle with absolute clarity.

[ BEAT 5: The Quiet Call to Action & Sign-Off ]
A natural, one-sentence transition to the offer (course, consulting, service).
Frictionless link + signature + optional conversational P.S.
```

### Example Micro-Lesson Subject Lines
- `You are not alone:`
- `This is what happened:`
- `Copywriting isn't math:`
- `How to write a compelling sentence (9 rules + 57 examples):`
- `The rules of "real" writing:`
- `The magic of writing anti-description:`

## 5. Pacing, Cadence, and Language Economy

Writing that converts moves with a distinct forward momentum. Eddie calls this the "Fast Feeling." It prevents the eye from sticking to the page.

### The Slippery Slide (Joe Sugarman)
The sole purpose of the first sentence is to get the reader to read the second sentence. The second sentence exists to pull them into the third. Every paragraph break, colon, and subhead functions as an incline on the slide.

### The Rule of 26 & Trimming
- **Cut dead syllables:** Replace noun phrases with active verbs (e.g., change *"conduct an investigation"* to *"investigate"*).
- **Delete decorative adjectives:** If an adjective does not add necessary factual context, delete it.
- **Vary sentence lengths:** A three-word sentence. Followed by a medium sentence that builds anticipation. Then an extended, descriptive sentence that sweeps the reader through the full consequence of the idea, landing on a single punchy closing line.
- **Read aloud:** If your tongue trips, your reader's eye will stop. Rewrite until the spoken cadence is effortless.

## 6. Person (Sans "a"): Customer Research Doctrine

Traditional marketing relies on the "Buyer Persona": an artificial composite filled with demographic abstractions (e.g., *"Marketing Mary, age 34, drinks iced lattes"*).

Eddie advocates for **Person (sans "a")**:
- Speak with one specific, living customer at a time.
- Conduct recorded qualitative interviews using Chris Voss negotiation mirroring (repeat the last 1 to 3 words with an inquisitive inflection).
- Note their verbatim frustration language, metaphors, and unprompted objections.
- Write the copy directly to that individual human being, addressing their singular doubts.

***

## 7. Compliance Checklist

1. **Zero Em-Dashes:** Replace em-dashes with colons, parentheses, commas, or separate sentences.
2. **Anti-Description Check:** Are there excess adjectives cluttering the mental movie? Trim to actions and dialogue.
3. **Fascination Filter Variety:** Do bullets alternate syntactical patterns without repeating the same opener?
4. **Work/Transaction Word Audit:** Ensure *learn*, *study*, *buy*, and *purchase* are purged from body and bullets.
5. **Fast Feeling Pacing:** Read the copy aloud. Does every line accelerate the slide into the next?
