# Audience Research: Person (sans "a") vs. Persona

Most marketing teams base landing pages on generic **buyer personas**—fictional composites cobbled together with demographic generalizations (*"Marketing Mary, 34, drives a Subaru, likes yoga, wants efficiency"*). 

Eddie Shleyner reveals that basing conversion copy on a Frankenstein persona is a critical mistake. To write copy that pierces the heart, you must write to a **Person (sans 'a')**—a single, living, breathing individual whose raw feelings represent thousands of others.

---

## The Andy Case Study: Discovering the Core Hook

When preparing the landing page for Claude Hopkins' classic *Scientific Advertising*, Eddie interviewed a junior copywriter named Andy. 

Instead of asking polite marketing survey questions, Eddie asked one direct, open-ended question:
> *"What's your biggest problem?"*

Andy's unfiltered response revealed the breakthrough emotional reality:
> *"I'm an imposter. I'm not suffering from imposter syndrome. I'm not being self-conscious. I am actually a fucking fake. My LinkedIn says I'm a copywriter. My website says I write copy. But I know in my heart that I don't know what I'm doing when I sit down to write an ad. And it's terrifying. I'm taking people's money and winging it... That's my biggest problem. I don't know what I'm doing."*

From Andy's raw vulnerability came the winning Hero copy:
- **Headline**: *"Write copy, confidently."*
- **Subhead**: *"Stop feeling like an imposter. Start thinking, writing, and selling like a professional conversion copywriter."*

## How to Conduct the Personal Interview

### 1. Identify an Authentic Representative
Find someone currently living in the beginner, intermediate, or advanced state your product solves. 

### 2. Record the Conversation
Never type notes while your subject speaks. Note-taking disrupts intimacy and makes the subject self-edit. Record the session (with permission) and transcribe it later.

### 3. Ask Open-Ended Questions
Start broad to let the subject direct the narrative:
- *"What's your biggest frustration right now?"*
- *"What keeps you up at night regarding [topic]?"*
- *"What was the exact moment you realized you needed help?"*

### 4. Use Verbal Mirroring (The Chris Voss Technique)
When the subject reaches an emotional statement, repeat their last 2 to 4 words back to them with an upward inflection:
- *Subject*: *"I feel like I'm just winging it every day."*
- *Interviewer*: *"Winging it every day?"*
- *Subject*: *"Yeah! Because nobody ever taught me how to test an ad..."*

Mirroring subconsciously encourages the subject to elaborate, digging below intellectualized talking points into raw, conversion-grade emotional truth.
