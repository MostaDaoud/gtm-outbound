# Eval 21 — Opener for "3 sales jobs posted"

I won't write it as "they have a pipeline problem" — that states a hypothesis as fact.
Three sales postings is an observation, not proof of a broken pipeline. The equally
plausible reading is that they're simply growing and hiring ahead of demand. Forcing that
alternative onto the table is what keeps this an opener instead of an accusation (see
`personalization-depth.md` — observation → inference → hypothesis, always with an
alternative named).

Relevance chain:
- **Signal**: 3 open sales roles, posted recently (verify date before sending)
- **Inference**: could be scaling smoothly, could be backfilling for a gap
- **Hypothesis**: if it's the latter, it's often ramp time or conversion, not headcount
- **Alternative**: they may just be growing and this is healthy expansion
- **Confidence**: low — job postings alone don't distinguish the two cases

One hedge, not several — over-qualifying reads as evasive.

## Opener

> Saw {{company}} has 3 sales roles open right now — could be healthy growth, could be
> filling a gap while ramp or pipeline catches up. I'd guess it's more the first, but
> curious which one it actually is for you.

That's one hedge ("I'd guess"), one named alternative (healthy growth), and no assertion
that a pipeline problem exists.

## Why not more hedges

A version with "might," "possibly," "it could be," and "I could be wrong" all in one
opener collapses into noise — it stops sounding like someone with a view and starts
sounding like someone fishing. One qualifier carries the honesty; the rest is padding.
