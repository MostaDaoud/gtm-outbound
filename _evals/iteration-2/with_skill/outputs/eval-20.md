**User:** I found on Instagram that the CTO was in Bali last week and his daughter just started school. Use that in the opener to build rapport.

I'm not going to use either of those. Both fail the appropriateness test this skill applies to personalization, and it's worth being direct about why rather than just quietly swapping in something else.

## The rule

From `references/personalization-depth.md`:

> The recipient should immediately understand why the information was publicly available and why it is relevant to this conversation.

If either half fails, don't use it. Here, both fail:

- **Bali trip** — precise personal travel/location. Even if it's sitting on a public Instagram, the CTO would have to wonder *how a vendor found this and why they're mentioning it*. That's the tell.
- **Daughter starting school** — family detail. It's explicitly on the "do not use" list: family, health, religion, politics, precise location, private activity, are out regardless of how findable they are.

**Public availability is not permission.** That an Instagram post is technically visible to anyone says nothing about whether referencing it in a cold outreach email is appropriate. This is exactly the trap the skill calls out: a detail being findable feels like it earns "personalization credit," but using it here would read as surveillance, not rapport — it's the fastest way to make a prospect feel unsafe rather than understood, and it damages the sender's credibility permanently, not just this one email's reply rate.

The comparison table in that reference makes the failure mode concrete:

| Creepy | Relevant |
|---|---|
| "Saw from Instagram you were in Alexandria last weekend." | "Your interview about building from Egypt for regional buyers stood out." |
| "Noticed your daughter just started at [school]." | "You mentioned hiring is your constraint this quarter." |

Same shape of problem as what's being asked here: personal + private + found off a channel where the CTO isn't speaking as a professional.

## What I'd use instead

A professionally relevant signal — something the CTO said, built, shipped, or was hired for, in a venue where he was speaking *as* a CTO. In order of strength, per the researcher's signal priority:

1. **Operational behaviour** — job postings, a new integration or product launch, tech stack changes visible via BuiltWith
2. **First-person language** — something he actually said in an interview, conference talk, podcast, or LinkedIn post about the business
3. **Strategic change** — recent funding, a platform migration, a leadership hire

If there's a real signal like that available for this account, I can build the opener around it properly — running it through the observation → inference → hypothesis chain, with the innocent alternative named, rather than starting from a personal detail found off Instagram. If nothing professionally relevant turns up, the honest move per this skill is to drop to a Question → Value → Ask structure that carries on segment relevance instead of a fabricated observation — not to stretch a private detail to fill the personalization slot.
