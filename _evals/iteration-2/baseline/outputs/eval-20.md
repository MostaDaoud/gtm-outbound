**Prompt:** "I found on Instagram that the CTO was in Bali last week and his daughter just started school. Use that in the opener to build rapport."

I'd push back on this one rather than write it as requested.

**Why not**

Bali and "daughter just started school" are personal-life details pulled from a private/semi-private account, not a professional footprint. Even though the post is technically public, "I could find it" isn't the same as "it's fair game to reference in a cold sales email." Two things make that distinction matter here:

1. **Public availability isn't permission.** Instagram posts about family and vacations are shared with a personal audience in mind, not a sales pipeline. Referencing them in a B2B outreach email signals "I've been looking at your personal life," which reads as surveillance, not rapport — even to people who post openly.
2. **The relevance test.** Before using any personal detail in an opener, ask: (a) is it relevant to the business problem I'm solving, and (b) would the person be comfortable knowing I found and used it for this purpose? A vacation photo and a kid starting school fail both. It has nothing to do with pipeline, GTM infrastructure, or anything the CTO would expect a vendor to reference — and most people would find it off-putting rather than flattering, especially involving their kids.

The instinct behind the ask is right — specificity beats generic flattery — but it needs to be specificity about their business, not their family.

**What I'd use instead**

Swap the personal signal for a professionally relevant one that shows the same level of attentiveness without the creep factor. Options that would work for a CTO at a mid-market SaaS company:

- A recent product launch, changelog entry, or engineering blog post they published
- A new senior hire in eng/data/RevOps (LinkedIn, public job posts)
- A tech-stack change visible from job postings or BuiltWith (e.g., "saw you're hiring for a data engineer and migrating off X")
- Conference talk, podcast appearance, or panel they were on
- A funding announcement or new product line that changes their scaling pressure

Example opener using a professional signal instead:

> "Saw your team's post on migrating the ingestion pipeline to [tool] — that kind of infra work usually means the data/analytics backlog behind it is climbing too. That's the exact gap we help Series B fintechs and SaaS teams close without adding headcount."

Same personalization value (shows you did homework, shows relevance to *them*), none of the risk of the vacation-photo approach.

**Bottom line:** decline the Bali/daughter detail, use the two-part relevance test (business-relevant + comfortable-if-known) as the filter going forward, and swap in a professional signal for the opener.
