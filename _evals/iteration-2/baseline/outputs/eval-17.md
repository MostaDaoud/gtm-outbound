# Outbound campaign: $40k ACV compliance platform → Heads of Risk, mid-market banks

Good fit for outbound — high ACV, a clearly identifiable and reachable buyer persona, a considered/complex purchase with a real business case, and a title that's findable on LinkedIn/Sales Nav and in compliance-conference attendee lists. This is exactly the profile outbound is built for. Let's build it.

## 1. ICP and targeting

**Firmographic filters:**
- Mid-market banks / credit unions / regional banks — roughly $1B–$50B in assets (adjust based on your actual sweet spot from closed-won deals)
- Regulated financial institutions subject to the compliance regime your platform addresses (BSA/AML, OFAC, GDPR, DORA, whatever your specific coverage is)
- Geography matched to your regulatory coverage

**Persona filters:**
- Primary: Head of Risk, Chief Risk Officer, VP Risk Management
- Secondary/influencer titles worth layering in as multi-threaded contacts: Head of Compliance, CCO, Head of Internal Audit, VP Regulatory Affairs
- Trigger events to prioritize: recent regulatory action/fine in the news, new CRO hire (new leaders re-evaluate vendors in the first 90-180 days), M&A activity (integration creates compliance gaps), recent examination findings if publicly reported

**List size target:** For a $40k ACV deal with a considered sales cycle, plan for roughly 150-300 accounts in the initial wave, 1-2 contacts per account (Head of Risk + one multi-thread contact), so ~250-500 people. Quality and account-fit matter far more than volume at this ACV.

## 2. Offer / angle

At $40k ACV to a risk buyer, lead with the cost of inaction and specificity, not generic value props. Strong angles:

- A specific regulatory trigger ("the updated [regulation] guidance effective [date] requires X — here's how banks your size are handling it")
- A peer proof point ("we helped [similar bank type] cut exam prep time by X% / reduce false-positive alerts by Y%")
- A quantified risk-reduction or efficiency number, since risk buyers respond to numbers over adjectives

## 3. Sequence structure (6-8 touches over ~3 weeks)

- **Email 1** — Under 80 words. One specific, credible observation about their bank/segment + one proof point + one soft ask (not "book a demo" — something lower-friction like "worth a 15-minute conversation?").
- **LinkedIn connection/comment** — Day 2-3, no pitch, just visibility.
- **Email 2** — Day 4-5. Different angle (a regulatory trigger or peer result), still short.
- **Call** — Day 6-7, timed to land right after email 2.
- **Email 3 (breakup-style)** — Day 10-12. Short, direct, gives them an easy out while leaving the door open.
- **LinkedIn message** — Day 12-14, more direct ask.
- **Final email** — Day 18-21. Genuine "closing the loop" note, low pressure.

Each touch should reference something specific to risk/compliance work — regulatory calendar, exam cycles, industry reports — not generic SaaS language.

## 4. Infrastructure and deliverability

- Separate sending domains from your primary corporate domain (subdomains or lookalike domains), warmed for 2-3 weeks before volume ramps
- Cap sends around 30-50/day per mailbox once warmed; scale total volume by adding domains/mailboxes, not by pushing per-mailbox volume up
- SPF, DKIM, and DMARC configured and monitored; one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058) headers in place given current Gmail/Yahoo bulk sender requirements
- Track and hold complaint rate below 0.1% (well under the 0.3% enforcement threshold) and bounce rate under 2%

## 5. Metrics to watch

- Reply rate (target 8-15% for a well-targeted, well-personalized B2B sequence at this ACV)
- Positive reply / meeting-booked rate off replies
- Meetings booked → SQL rate
- Cost per booked meeting and cost per opportunity, tracked from day one so you know your unit economics early
