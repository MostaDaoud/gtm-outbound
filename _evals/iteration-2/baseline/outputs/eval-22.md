**Prompt:** "Here's my draft: 'Hi {{first_name}}, I hope this email finds you well. I came across your profile and was really impressed. Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call?' Make it better."

Here's my read on why this draft is underperforming, then a rewrite.

**What's wrong with it — three separate problems**

1. **"I hope this email finds you well."** Pure ceremony. It's the cold-email equivalent of throat-clearing — costs you the first (and most valuable) line of the email, signals mass-send/template energy, and gives the reader zero reason to keep reading. Should be cut entirely; open with substance instead.

2. **"I came across your profile and was really impressed."** Generic admiration that isn't personalization — it's a personalization-shaped placeholder. "Impressed" by what, specifically? If you can't finish that sentence with a concrete, verifiable detail about their business, it reads as flattery-by-mail-merge and most recipients have seen it enough times to pattern-match it instantly.

3. **"Would you be open to a quick 15-minute call?"** This is a high-friction ask for a cold, first-touch email. You're asking someone who doesn't know you yet to give up calendar time before you've established any value or trust. "Quick" and "15-minute" don't actually lower the friction — a stranger's calendar invite is a stranger's calendar invite. Reply rates on a first cold email consistently drop when the CTA is a meeting versus a lower-commitment next step.

**The fix — lower the friction, raise the specificity**

Rewrite:

> Subject: quick question about [specific, relevant trigger]
>
> Hi {{first_name}},
>
> [Specific, verifiable observation about their business — a hire, a launch, a tech-stack signal, a job posting, a funding event — one sentence, no fluff.]
>
> We helped a Series B fintech cut cost-per-booked-call 34% in 90 days by [one-line mechanism relevant to the trigger above].
>
> Worth a look, or should I send it your way instead of asking for time?

What changed:
- Cut the ceremonial opener entirely — first line now carries information, not politeness.
- Replaced "impressed" with a specific, checkable observation — this is the actual test of personalization: could you defend this sentence if the prospect asked "how do you know that?"
- Replaced the 15-minute-call ask with a lower-friction offer (send more info / reply with interest / a yes-no question) that gets a "yes" easier and still gives you a clear next step to escalate to a call once there's a signal of interest.

**One more thing worth flagging:** past a certain point, "does this read better" is a judgment call, and two people (or the same person on two days) can disagree. If you're iterating on drafts like this at scale, it's worth scoring variants against a consistent rubric — ceremonial-opener flags, personalization specificity, CTA friction level — rather than only eyeballing each version, so you're comparing them on the same axis instead of vibes.
