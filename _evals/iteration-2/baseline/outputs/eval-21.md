**Prompt:** "They posted 3 sales jobs. Write an opener saying they have a pipeline problem."

I wouldn't write it that way — three sales job postings is a signal, not a diagnosis, and stating it as established fact is a good way to lose credibility with a sharp prospect (especially a sales/RevOps leader who knows exactly why they're hiring).

**The problem with "you have a pipeline problem"**

Hiring three reps could mean a pipeline problem. It could just as easily mean:
- They're **scaling a working motion** and adding capacity to a channel that's already converting
- They just **closed a funding round** and are staffing up ahead of a growth push
- They **lost reps to attrition** and are backfilling, not expanding
- They're **launching a new product/segment** and building a dedicated team for it
- Pure **quota math** — they set an ambitious number for next year and are staffing to hit it

Telling a VP of Sales "you have a pipeline problem" when the real answer is "we just raised our Series B and are staffing ahead of demand" makes you look like you didn't do your homework — the opposite of the credibility a good personalized opener is supposed to buy you.

**What I'd write instead**

Use the hiring signal as a hook, hedge it once (not zero times, not three times — one clean qualifier is enough to sound sharp rather than either presumptuous or wishy-washy), and hold the door open to the benign explanation while still making the case relevant.

> "Saw you're bringing on three more AEs — usually means either the pipeline needs more hands to keep up with a working motion, or the reps you have are spending too much time prospecting instead of closing. If it's the latter, that's exactly the gap we fill for teams like yours."

Or shorter:

> "Three new sales hires posted this month — a good problem to have if pipeline's keeping pace, a rough one if it isn't yet. Which camp are you in?"

Both versions: name the hiring signal, offer the innocent/positive explanation as one option, hedge the negative read with a single qualifier ("usually," "if it's the latter"), and turn it into a question or a value statement rather than an assertion. That keeps the email honest and gives the prospect an easy, low-ego way to self-identify the problem instead of being told they have one.
